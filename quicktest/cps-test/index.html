How to use the CPS test

Pick a time limit — 1, 5, 10, or 60 seconds — then click or tap inside the box as fast as you can until time runs out. Your total clicks, live CPS (clicks per second), and remaining time all update in real time. Your final score is shown the moment the timer hits zero.

What counts as a good CPS score

Most people click at roughly 5–7 clicks per second using a normal single-finger clicking technique over a 5-second test. Competitive gamers using techniques like "jitter clicking" or "butterfly clicking" can reach 10–14+ CPS, though these trade accuracy and comfort for raw speed.

Why people use a CPS test

  • Gamers testing click speed for games where rapid clicking matters
  • Comparing click speed and consistency between different mice
  • Simply curious how their clicking speed compares to typical benchmarks

Frequently asked questions

What's a good CPS score?

Around 5–7 CPS is typical for normal clicking. Scores above 10 CPS usually involve specialized clicking techniques rather than a single finger tapping normally.

Does mouse type affect CPS?

Yes, to a degree — mice with lighter click resistance or faster switch response can allow slightly higher sustained CPS, though technique matters more than hardware for most people.

Why is my 1-second score higher than my 10-second score?

This is completely normal — an initial burst of speed is easier to sustain briefly than over a longer period, where hand fatigue naturally lowers your average rate.
